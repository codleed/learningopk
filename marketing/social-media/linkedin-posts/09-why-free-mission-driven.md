# LinkedIn Post #09: The Freemium Model — Why Access Matters

**Title:** LearningoPK's Freemium Model — Ad-Supported Free Tier, Affordable Subscriptions, and Why It Works

**Target Audience:** Both (schools + investors)

**Best Posting Time:** Friday, 8:00 AM PKT

## Post Body

Every time I describe LearningoPK to someone in the education sector, the second question — right after "How does the AI work?" — is: "How do you make money?"

It's a fair question. The first question. The one that determines whether this is a business or a charity.

LearningoPK is a business. But we ensure every student can access the platform regardless of their ability to pay. Let me explain why that isn't a contradiction — it's the core of the strategy.

**The economics of charging every student in Pakistan don't work.**

The median monthly household income in Pakistan is approximately Rs. 45,000 (~$160). A subscription model at even $3/month — roughly Rs. 850 — is a meaningful line item for most families. At $5/month, you've priced out the students who need you most.

But here's what most people miss: the students who can afford to pay are also the students who already have options — private schools, tuition centers, home tutors. The students who can't pay are the ones locked out of quality exam preparation entirely.

Charging every student means serving the students who need you least. That's a bad business model wrapped in a worse mission.

**The alternative: A sustainable freemium model.**

Here's how it works:

1. **Ad-supported free tier** — Students can access all core learning features free with education-appropriate display ads. Every student, regardless of financial background, can use the platform. The ads are screened, non-intrusive, and never interrupt quizzes or AI tutor sessions.

2. **Individual subscriptions at PKR 200/month** — Students who want an ad-free experience with premium features (unlimited AI tutoring, advanced analytics, unlimited mock exams) can subscribe. This converts power users who want the enhanced experience — entirely optional, never gating core content.

3. **School subscriptions at PKR 500/student/year** — Schools and institutions get ad-free access for all enrolled students, plus an institutional dashboard with analytics, progress tracking, and management tools. For a school of 500 students, that's PKR 250,000/year for a platform that meaningfully improves board exam results.

This isn't a new model. It's what Spotify does with ad-supported free listening and premium subscriptions. It's what YouTube does. Freemium — the free tier is the top of the funnel, subscriptions are the revenue engine.

**The mission isn't marketing. It's product design.**

When your free tier has ads, you have to build something so good that students choose to use it despite the ads — and so good that power users want to pay to remove them. You can't hide behind a paywall and call it a business. You have to win on quality.

That constraint makes the product better. Every feature has to justify its existence through actual student value — because students can always walk away.

**LearningoPK will make money. But we will never lock a 9th grader in a government school out of quality exam preparation.**

If that resonates with you — as an educator, an investor, or someone who remembers what board exam pressure feels like — I'd value the conversation.
