## B-Roll Suggestions

1. **Classroom establishing shots (0:00–0:15):** Stock footage or original footage of Pakistani classrooms — students in uniform, teachers at whiteboards/blackboards, exam halls. These establish the context and make the problem feel real to the viewer.

2. **Manual grading contrast (0:08–0:20):** Close-up of a teacher's hands marking papers with a red pen, a stack of unmarked papers, a tired teacher's expression. Contrast with the automated dashboard later in the video. The emotional contrast makes the value proposition visceral.

3. **Teacher → Dashboard transition (1:30–2:15):** If possible, film a real teacher using the dashboard for the first time — that moment of realization when they see student data they've never had before. Alternative: screen-record the dashboard with thoughtful "this is powerful" voiceover.

4. **"Time savings" visualization (2:35–2:45):** Animated clock running backwards, or a visual of hours being "recovered" — e.g., a pile of papers shrinking and a teacher walking into a classroom with more energy. Keep it simple.

5. **Map of Pakistan with schools (3:55–4:05):** Show a map of Pakistan with pin markers appearing in different cities — representing the 20 pilot schools. This visual reinforces the national scope and makes the viewer think "I want my school to be one of those pins."

6. **Principal/administrator testimonial (optional, 3:15–3:35):** If you have a pilot school already committed, insert a 15-second clip of the principal saying why they joined. Authentic social proof is worth more than any scripted pitch.

## Production Notes

- **Tone:** This is a B2B pitch video. The tone should be professional, confident, and data-driven — but not cold. Speak directly to the viewer as a peer who understands the challenges of running a Pakistani educational institution. Empathy first, solution second.
- **Presenter:** If using an on-camera presenter, dress professionally (business casual to formal — think school meeting attire). Speak directly to camera. Use confident, open body language. This video represents the brand to decision-makers.
- **Pacing:** Slightly slower and more deliberate than the student-facing videos. School administrators need time to absorb information. Pause briefly after key statistics and value propositions.
- **Data visualization:** Every statistic mentioned should have a supporting visual. Clean, simple charts and graphics. Avoid cluttered slides. One idea per visual.
- **Captions:** Burned-in English captions are essential — many viewers will be in environments where they can't use audio (staff rooms, offices).
- **Music:** Subtle, professional background track — corporate but not sterile. Think TED Talk background music. Duck significantly under voiceover.
- **Call to Action visibility:** The email and LinkedIn contact information should appear on screen during the relevant section (3:45–3:55) and remain visible in the end screen. Make it impossible for an interested viewer to miss.
