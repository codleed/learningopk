## 1. Brand Foundation

### 1.1 Mission Statement

LearningoPK exists to democratize exam preparation for every Pakistani secondary student. We deliver board-specific, AI-powered learning tools — with an ad-supported free tier accessible to all — so that a student's financial background never determines their academic future.

### 1.2 Vision Statement

A Pakistan where every 9th and 10th grade student, regardless of city, school type, or household income, walks into their board exams with the same preparation advantage that only expensive tuition centers currently provide. We envision LearningoPK becoming the default exam preparation layer for Pakistan's secondary education system — trusted by students, recommended by teachers, and adopted by schools.

### 1.3 Brand Promise

**We promise every Pakistani student:**

- **Board-specific accuracy** — Every chapter, quiz, and past paper is mapped to your exact syllabus (FBISE, Punjab BISE, Sindh BISE). No irrelevant content. No guesswork.
- **Instant, intelligent help** — A 24/7 AI tutor that teaches you how to think, not just what to memorize. Stuck at 2 AM before your physics exam? We're there.
- **Accessible to all** — Start learning free with ads. Upgrade to an ad-free premium experience at an affordable price. No bait-and-switch. Your data is never sold.
- **Gamified motivation** — Learning is hard. We make it rewarding with XP, streaks, leaderboards, and badges that celebrate effort, not just results.
- **Respect for your context** — Ramadan mode, localized examples, culturally relevant content. We're built for Pakistan, not adapted from somewhere else.

### 1.4 Brand Personality

LearningoPK's personality is defined by seven core traits. Every piece of content should express at least two of these.

| Trait | What It Means | What It Doesn't Mean |
|---|---|---|
| **Smart** | We understand pedagogy, technology, and the Pakistani exam system deeply. Our content reflects real expertise. | Arrogant. We never talk down to students or teachers. |
| **Empowering** | Everything we do makes students feel more capable, more confident, more ready. We're a hand up, not a handout. | Patronizing. We don't "save" anyone — we give them tools and trust them to use them. |
| **Trustworthy** | We deliver what we promise. Board-specific means board-specific. Free means free. Our AI is transparent about its limitations. | Boring. Trust doesn't require a monotone. |
| **Accessible** | Our language is clear, our platform is mobile-friendly, our signup takes 60 seconds. We remove friction everywhere. | Dumbed-down. Accessibility means clarity, not simplicity of thought. |
| **Modern** | We're a tech company. Our design is clean, our AI is cutting-edge, our approach is data-driven. We feel current. | Trendy-for-trendy's-sake. We don't chase design fads; we chase usability. |
| **Relentlessly Helpful** | Every feature, every piece of content, every communication adds value to a student's learning journey. If it doesn't help, we don't ship it. | Feature-bloated. Helpfulness is about impact, not quantity. |
| **Rooted** | We're proudly Pakistani. Our references, our examples, our cultural awareness (Ramadan mode, exam-season timing) reflect lived reality here. | Nationalistic. We're pro-Pakistan, not anti-anywhere-else. |

### 1.5 Brand Voice and Tone

**Voice is consistent. Tone adapts to context.**

Our **brand voice** is:

- **Confident, not cocky** — "Our platform covers 40+ board-mapped chapters across FBISE, Punjab, and Sindh." Not: "We're the best thing to ever happen to Pakistani education."
- **Clear, not clinical** — "Stuck on a chemistry problem? Ask the AI tutor — it'll guide you step by step." Not: "The Socratic dialogue interface facilitates conceptual resolution of stoichiometric queries."
- **Warm, not saccharine** — "We know exam season is stressful. Here's what we built to help." Not: "Don't worry, sweetie, everything will be okay!!!"
- **Direct, not abrupt** — "Sign up in 60 seconds. No credit card needed." Not: "Registration → Credential submission → Verification → Access granted."
- **Informed, not academic** — We cite data and research, but we don't write like a journal paper.

**Tone spectrum by context:**

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Website hero** | Bold, inviting | "Learn Smarter. Score Higher. Start free." |
| **Blog post** | Expert, conversational | "Here's why spaced repetition beats cramming every single time." |
| **Social media** | Energetic, relatable | "That moment when the past paper question matches exactly what you revised. 😤" |
| **Investor pitch** | Confident, data-backed | "Pakistan's tuition market is $3.2B annually. 78% of secondary students pay for private tutoring. LearningoPK makes that spend unnecessary." |
| **Error message** | Helpful, human | "Looks like something went wrong. Try refreshing — if that doesn't fix it, our team's already on it." |
| **Email newsletter** | Personal, value-first | "Hey [Name] — we analyzed 5 years of FBISE physics papers. Here's what keeps appearing." |
| **In-app copy** | Encouraging, brief | "3-day streak! You're building a serious habit. 🔥" |
| **Customer support** | Empathetic, solution-oriented | "I understand the confusion — let me walk you through how chapter bookmarks work." |

**Voice do's and don'ts:**

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Use "you" and "your" — speak directly to the reader | Use "users," "learners," "individuals" — it's distancing |
| Write short sentences. Then even shorter ones. | Write paragraph-length sentences with nested clauses that lose the reader halfway through. |
| Use Pakistani English conventions (programme → program, colour → colour, organise → organize) | Mix American and British spellings inconsistently |
| Use contractions (we're, it's, you'll) | Write like a government memo |
| Lead with the benefit, then explain the feature | Lead with the feature and hope the reader infers the benefit |
| Acknowledge difficulty ("This chapter is tough. Here's our approach.") | Pretend everything is easy — students know better |

### 3.6 Dark Mode / Light Mode Usage Rules

**Default experience: Dark mode.** LearningoPK is a study platform. Students study at night. Dark mode reduces eye strain, saves battery on mobile devices, and differentiates our brand visually.

**Light mode availability:** Users can toggle to light mode. Light mode follows the same design system with the light palette values. It exists for accessibility and user preference — not as an equal alternative.

**Marketing rules:**
- **Website, social media graphics, video content:** Dark mode aesthetics by default
- **Print materials, pitch decks, one-pagers:** Light mode (for readability on paper)
- **Email templates:** Light mode (email clients render light backgrounds more reliably)
- **Investor presentations:** Designer's choice based on context — light for printed decks, dark for projected presentations

**Transition behavior:** The toggle between modes must be instant (no animation delay) and must persist across sessions. Never flash a white screen during dark-to-dark navigation.

## 4. Content Style Guide

### 4.1 Writing Principles

**Principle 1: Student-first, not platform-first.**
Every sentence should answer "what does this mean for the student?" before "what does this say about us?"

- **Platform-first:** "LearningoPK features an advanced SM-2 spaced repetition algorithm."
- **Student-first:** "Our flashcards show you the exact moment you're about to forget something — so you review it just in time to lock it into long-term memory."

**Principle 2: Specific beats vague.**
Numbers, names, and concrete details build trust. Generalities erode it.

- **Vague:** "We cover all major Pakistani exam boards."
- **Specific:** "We cover FBISE, Punjab BISE, and Sindh BISE — every chapter, every subject, for both 9th and 10th grade."

**Principle 3: Show the mechanism.**
Don't just claim something works — explain how. This builds credibility with skeptical audiences (parents, investors, technically-literate students).

- **Claim only:** "Our AI tutor helps students learn better."
- **Show mechanism:** "Our AI tutor uses the Socratic method — instead of giving you the answer, it asks guiding questions that lead you to figure it out yourself. That's the difference between memorizing and understanding."

**Principle 4: One idea per paragraph.**
Resist the urge to pack multiple concepts into dense paragraphs. Digital reading is scanning. Make every paragraph earn its scroll.

**Principle 5: Pakistani English conventions.**
- Use **-ise** not **-ize** (organise, prioritise, recognise)
- Use **-our** not **-or** (colour, behaviour, favour)
- Use **programme** (education programme) not **program** (unless referring to computer program)
- Use **maths** not **math**
- Use British date format: 15 May 2026
- Use PKR for currency, with commas: PKR 3,000 (not Rs. 3000 or 3000 PKR)
- Spell out numbers one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above

### 4.2 Grammar and Mechanics Preferences

| Rule | Our Style |
|---|---|
| **Oxford comma** | Yes. "Physics, Chemistry, and Biology" not "Physics, Chemistry and Biology." |
| **Em dashes** | Yes — with no spaces on either side — for parenthetical emphasis. |
| **Ellipses** | Sparingly. Use only to indicate a pause or trailing thought in conversational content. Never in formal copy. |
| **Quotation marks** | Single quotes for UI labels ('Submit'), double quotes for speech and citations. |
| **Capitalization in headings** | Sentence case for all headings (including H1). "How the AI tutor works" not "How the AI Tutor Works." The brand name LearningoPK is always title case. |
| **Bullet points** | No periods at the end unless the bullet is a full sentence. Consistent parallel structure across all bullets in a list. |
| **Links** | Descriptive anchor text. "Read our exam pattern analysis" not "Click here to read more." |
| **Percentages** | Use the % symbol: 78%, not 78 percent. |
| **Time** | 12-hour format with AM/PM: 8:00 PM, not 20:00. |

### 4.4 How to Talk About the AI Tutor

**Critical rule: Do not anthropomorphize the AI.**

The AI tutor is a tool — sophisticated, helpful, and intelligent — but it is not a person. Anthropomorphizing creates false expectations, trust issues when the AI makes mistakes, and ethical concerns around student-AI relationships.

| Do Say | Don't Say |
|---|---|
| "The AI tutor guides you through problems step by step." | "Meet your new AI study buddy!" |
| "Ask the AI tutor any question about your syllabus." | "Your personal AI teacher knows exactly what you need." |
| "The AI tutor uses the Socratic method to help you think through problems." | "Our AI is like having a friendly teacher in your pocket 24/7." |
| "The AI tutor is powered by Mistral AI and trained on board-specific content." | "Our brilliant AI tutor understands you." |
| "If the AI tutor gives an unclear response, try rephrasing your question." | "Sometimes the AI gets confused — it's still learning!" (Implies consciousness) |

**Transparency requirements:**
- Always disclose that the tutor is AI-powered. "AI tutor" is our standard term.
- In the platform, clearly label AI-generated content: "Generated by AI — review carefully."
- Never claim the AI is infallible. Use language like "aims to," "designed to," "helps with."
- The AI does not have feelings, opinions, or a relationship with the student. Our copy must never imply otherwise.

### 4.5 How to Talk About the Freemium Model

**Critical rule: Be transparent about the model.**

Pakistani consumers are rightly skeptical of products that claim to be "free" and later introduce charges. Our messaging must be upfront about our freemium model.

**The framework:**

1. **State the model clearly:** "Start free with ads. Upgrade for the full experience."
2. **Explain immediately:** "Students can learn free with education-appropriate ads. Subscriptions at PKR 200/month remove ads and unlock premium features."
3. **Address the hidden-catch fear:** "No credit card required to start. Your data is never sold."

| Do Say | Don't Say |
|---|---|
| "Start free with ads — no credit card required." | "Free to start!" (Implies future charges without explanation) |
| "Subscribe for PKR 200/month to remove ads and unlock premium." | "Try it free." (Implying a trial period) |
| "Funded by ad revenue and subscriptions, so students always have a free option." | "We're free because we care." (Vague, raises more questions) |
| "Sign up in 60 seconds. No payment info needed." | "Free tier available." (Implies everything else is paid) |

**When announcing new features:**
Frame new features as the natural fulfillment of our mission — accessible to all students through the free tier, with enhanced versions for subscribers.

- **Good:** "Formula library is now live — because every student deserves organized, searchable access to every formula they'll need."
- **Good (for premium):** "Unlimited AI tutoring sessions are now available — included in the PKR 200/month subscription."

## 5. Social Media Style

### 5.1 Profile Image Guidelines

**Primary avatar (all platforms):** The LearningoPK icon — indigo symbol on a dark background (`#0F0F23` or near-black).

- The icon must be centered with 20% internal padding
- Use the icon-only variant, not the wordmark (wordmarks are illegible at avatar sizes)
- Same avatar across all platforms for instant recognition
- Update the avatar no more than once per year (consistency > novelty)

**Platform-specific sizes:**
- LinkedIn: 400 × 400 px
- Instagram: 320 × 320 px
- YouTube: 800 × 800 px
- Facebook: 360 × 360 px
- X/Twitter: 400 × 400 px

### 5.2 Post Visual Style by Platform

#### LinkedIn

- **Format:** Text-first posts with optional single supporting image (screenshot, chart, infographic)
- **Image style:** Clean, high-contrast screenshots of the platform or data visualizations in brand colors. Dark backgrounds preferred.
- **Text style:** Long-form native posts (1,200–2,000 characters). No external link in the main post body (add link in comments to avoid reach penalty).
- **Carousel documents:** PDF carousels with one idea per slide, indigo/white/dark color scheme, Syne headings, DM Sans body. 8–12 slides optimal.
- **Typography on images:** Syne for any text overlaid on graphics. Never use script or decorative fonts.

#### Instagram

- **Feed posts:** Three formats only:
  1. **Carousels (primary):** Educational content — stats, tips, feature breakdowns. 5–10 slides. First slide is the hook (bold stat or question). Brand colors throughout. Consistent design template.
  2. **Single images (secondary):** Platform screenshots, motivational quotes, announcements. Always include the LearningoPK watermark/icon in the corner.
  3. **Reels (growth):** Quick platform demos, exam tips, relatable student content. 15–45 seconds. CapCut-edited with subtitles. Vertical 9:16.
- **Grid aesthetic:** Dark backgrounds dominate. Every 6th post can be a lighter/quote post for visual rhythm.
- **Typography on graphics:** DM Sans for body text, Syne for headlines. Never use more than 2 fonts on a single graphic.

#### YouTube

- **Thumbnails:** High contrast. One focal element (person reacting, platform screenshot, bold text). Indigo + amber color scheme. Syne for any text (max 5 words). 1280 × 720 px. No clickbait faces or misleading arrows.
- **Video intro:** 3-second branded intro animation (LearningoPK icon + tagline). Consistent across all videos.
- **Video outro:** 10-second end screen with subscribe CTA + 2 video cards. Dark background with indigo accent.
- **Lower thirds:** DM Sans, indigo bar, white text. Used for speaker names, feature labels, chapter names.

### 5.3 Story / Highlight Cover Style

**Instagram Stories:**
- Behind-the-scenes content, quick polls, quiz questions, feature teasers, student takeovers
- Use the brand color palette for backgrounds
- Syne for any text overlays
- Always include a swipe-up/link sticker when available
- Interactive stickers (poll, quiz, question) in every other story

**Instagram Highlight Covers:**
- Custom circular icons for each highlight category
- Categories: Features, AI Tutor, Quizzes, Community, Behind the Scenes, Exam Tips, Press
- Indigo circle background with white icon, 20% internal padding
- Consistent icon style (Lucide Icons, 1.5px stroke)

### 5.4 Emoji Usage Policy

**General rule:** Emojis are permitted in social media, community interactions, and in-app gamification messages. They are not permitted in website copy, investor communications, white papers, press releases, or email subjects to professional audiences.

**Permitted contexts:**
- Social media captions and comments (all platforms)
- In-app achievement notifications and gamification UI
- Community forum posts and replies
- WhatsApp/chat-based communication
- Student-facing email newsletters (sparingly)

**Prohibited contexts:**
- Website hero text, feature descriptions, or any public-facing product copy
- Investor pitch decks, one-pagers, or formal communications
- Press releases and media statements
- B2B/school-facing emails and proposals
- Blog post titles and body text (except in quoted social media examples)
- Error messages and system notifications

**Approved emojis (use these freely):**
🔥 Streaks and progress | 📚 Studying and content | 💡 Tips and insights | 🎯 Goals and targets | ⚡ Quick actions | 🏆 Achievements | ✅ Completion | 📊 Analytics and data | ⏰ Time and deadlines | 🧠 Learning and cognition

**Approved emojis (use sparingly, context-dependent):**
😤 Determination/frustration (exam stress content) | 😅 Relief (post-exam content) | 🎉 Celebration (major milestones only) | 💪 Encouragement | 👀 Attention/"check this out"

**Prohibited emojis:**
😂 (overused, unprofessional), 🤣 (same), 🥺 (infantilizing to students), 🙏 (religious connotation — avoid in brand communications unless specifically Ramadan-related context), 💀 (negative connotation), 🔫💣💰🍺 (obviously inappropriate)

**Emoji placement rules:**
- In captions: At the end of paragraphs or as bullet-point markers. Never mid-sentence.
- Maximum 5 emojis per social media caption. Less is more.
- No emoji-only communication. Emojis supplement words, they don't replace them.

## 7. Governance

### 7.1 Brand Stewardship

The founder or designated brand lead has final authority on all brand decisions. No team member should create public-facing content that hasn't been reviewed against these guidelines until they've internalized them through consistent use.

### 7.2 Updates to These Guidelines

This document is versioned and reviewed quarterly. As the platform evolves (new boards, new features, new audiences), these guidelines must evolve too. Proposed changes should be discussed and documented before implementation.

### 7.3 Questions and Edge Cases

When facing a brand decision not covered by these guidelines, ask:
1. Does this choice serve the student?
2. Does it build or erode trust?
3. Is it consistent with how we've shown up before?
4. Would our most skeptical audience member believe it?

If the answer to any of these is "no," reconsider the choice.
